\cvsection{Research statement}

I build inference methods for terrestrial ecosystems where the measurement is indirect, the model
is structurally wrong, and the field campaign is small. My group works on three linked problems:
propagating uncertainty from raw reflectance through to carbon flux estimates, deciding where to
send a field team when the travel budget binds, and detecting when an ecosystem model is right for
the wrong reason. The methods are developed against long-running sites in temperate mixed forest
and are released as open software.

\cvsection{Software and data}

\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.2em,itemsep=3pt,topsep=2pt]
  \item \textbf{canopyfit}. Radiative transfer inversion with physically constrained priors.
    Approximately 4{,}100 downloads per month, 61 external contributors, used in the reprocessing
    pipeline at two national observatories.
  \item \textbf{transect-tools}. Field campaign planning under travel and labour budgets. Adopted
    by the Terrestrial Inference Network as its reference planner.
  \item \textbf{Marchwood transect archive}. Twelve years of paired lidar, spectral, and
    destructive-sample records from 42 plots, released annually under an open licence.
\end{itemize}
