Recovering From the Build: The Interruption Cost of\\
Compile Notifications in Developer Tooling}

\begin{abstract}
\noindent
\footnotesize
Compilation is slow enough in most serious toolchains that developers do
something else while they wait, and the tool has to tell them when the build is
done. Nearly every editor answers this with an immediate notification, on the
implicit theory that a developer who has moved on wants to know at once. We
tested that theory. In a within-subjects laboratory study with 36 professional
developers, each completing four instrumented editing tasks under four
notification conditions, immediate toasts produced the worst outcome on every
measure we recorded. Mean resumption lag after a compile notification was
14.2~s under immediate toasts against 6.9~s under an ambient gutter marker, a
difference of 7.3~s with a 95 percent confidence interval of [5.4, 9.2] and a
large effect (\cohensd{} = 1.12). Task completion was 18 percent slower and
self-reported mental demand 21 points higher on a 100-point scale. The ambient
condition beat the silent control as well, so the finding is not simply that
notifications are bad; it is that a notification which demands a gaze shift and
a dismissal action costs more than the information it delivers is worth. We
give a design recommendation, an estimate of when it stops holding, and the
instrumentation we used, which is reusable in other editors.
\end{abstract}
