\begin{abstract}
Nitrogen availability sets the ceiling on primary production across much of the
temperate shelf, yet the transcriptional program a diatom runs when nitrogen
becomes limiting is only partly resolved. We cultured
\emph{Thalassiosira pseudonana} strain \strain{} in semi-continuous batch under
nitrogen-replete and nitrogen-limited regimes, sampled at mid-exponential phase,
and sequenced six stranded polyadenylated libraries to a median depth of
34.2 million read pairs. After adapter trimming and rRNA depletion in silico,
92.4 percent of reads aligned to the reference assembly. Differential expression
testing recovered 1{,}486 transcripts at a false discovery rate below 0.05, of
which 863 were induced. Nitrate and ammonium transporters, glutamine synthetase
III, and the urea cycle carbamoyl phosphate synthase dominated the induced set,
while photosystem II antenna proteins and ribosomal proteins were the strongest
repressed group. Two libraries showed elevated optical duplication, and we report
the quality control evidence that led us to keep them rather than resequence.
\end{abstract}
