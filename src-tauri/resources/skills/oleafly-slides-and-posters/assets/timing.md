# Timing

## Slide count by talk length

Roughly one slide per minute. A dense data slide takes two to three minutes, a section divider takes fifteen seconds, and a title slide takes none.

| Length | Slides | Title and intro | Methods | Results | Discussion | Conclusion |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 5 min | 5 to 7 | 1 to 2 | 0 to 1 | 2 to 3 | 1 | 1 |
| 10 min | 10 to 12 | 2 | 1 to 2 | 4 to 5 | 2 to 3 | 1 |
| 15 min | 15 to 18 | 2 to 3 | 2 to 3 | 6 to 8 | 3 to 4 | 1 to 2 |
| 20 min | 20 to 24 | 3 | 3 to 4 | 8 to 10 | 4 to 5 | 2 |
| 30 min | 25 to 30 | 3 to 4 | 5 to 6 | 10 to 12 | 6 to 8 | 2 |
| 45 min | 35 to 45 | 4 to 5 | 8 to 10 | 15 to 20 | 8 to 10 | 2 to 3 |
| 60 min | 45 to 60 | 5 to 6 | 10 to 12 | 20 to 25 | 10 to 12 | 3 to 4 |

Overlay pages made with `\pause` are not slides. A frame with four `\pause` commands is one slide and five PDF pages.

## The 15 minute conference talk

The default academic slot, usually followed by two to five minutes of questions.

```
Introduction        2 to 3 min    2 to 3 slides
  Title and hook       30 s
  Background            90 s
  Research question     60 s

Methods             2 to 3 min    2 to 3 slides
  Design                60 to 90 s
  Key procedure         60 s
  Analysis              30 to 60 s

Results             6 to 7 min    6 to 8 slides
  Primary finding       2 to 3 min
  Secondary finding     2 min
  Validation            2 min

Discussion          2 to 3 min    3 to 4 slides
  Interpretation        60 s
  Prior work            60 s
  Implications          60 s

Conclusion          1 min         1 to 2 slides
  Takeaways             45 s
  Acknowledgements      15 s
```

Say the main finding early. An audience that knows where the talk is going follows the method; an audience waiting for a reveal stops listening at minute six.

Spend no more than two minutes on background. Everyone in the room already knows why the field exists.

## The 45 minute seminar

More time means more depth on the method and more room for the argument, not more results. The audience came to understand how you did it.

- Give the method 8 to 10 slides instead of 2.
- Use section divider slides. The audience needs the map.
- Build in two or three natural pause points where a question can land.
- Keep 5 to 10 minutes for discussion even if the slot does not formally reserve it.

## The thesis defense

- The committee has read the thesis. Do not summarize it chapter by chapter.
- Structure around the contributions, one per section.
- Prepare a deep backup section: every table, every ablation, every derivation the committee might ask about. Twenty backup slides for a forty-slide talk is normal.
- Rehearse the transitions between chapters. That is where a defense stalls.

## Practising

- Time the whole talk out loud at least once. Reading silently takes about 70 percent of the time speaking does.
- Mark the slide you should be on at the halfway point, and check yourself against it.
- Build in a 10 percent buffer. Talks run long, never short.
- Know which two slides you can drop if the session is behind. Decide in advance, not on the podium.
- Going over time takes minutes from the next speaker and is the one delivery mistake an audience actually resents.
