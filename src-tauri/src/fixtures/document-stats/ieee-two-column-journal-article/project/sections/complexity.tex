\section{Complexity}
\label{sec:complexity}

The channel update costs one multiplication by $\mat{X}^{\mathsf{H}}$, which is
a fast Fourier transform at $O(N \log N)$, plus a solve against a fixed
$L \times L$ precision matrix that is Cholesky factorised once at
$O(L^3)$ and reused. The smoother is a scalar Kalman recursion at $O(N)$.
Projecting the leakage onto the $R$-dimensional dominant subspace of
Proposition~\ref{prop:rank} costs $O(N R^2)$ per iteration. The total is
therefore
\begin{equation}
  \label{eq:complexity}
  O\!\left( I \left[ N \log N + N R^{2} \right] + L^{3} \right)
\end{equation}
for $I$ iterations, against $O(N^3)$ for forming and inverting the whitened
least squares system directly, and $O(N \log N)$ for the common-phase baseline.

Table~\ref{tab:complexity} instantiates Eq.~\eqref{eq:complexity} at the
parameters of Section~\ref{sec:results}. The measured figures come from a
fixed-point implementation on a commercial baseband core at 800~MHz, counting
only the estimation kernel. \est{} costs 3.4 times the common-phase baseline
and roughly one two-hundredth of the direct whitened solve, which is the
comparison that matters, because the direct solve is the estimator \est{}
approximates.

Memory is dominated by the $N \times R$ subspace basis, 36~kB in single
precision at these parameters, which is small enough to keep resident across
symbols. The basis depends only on $\beta T_s N$, so it is recomputed only when
the oscillator temperature drifts enough to move the linewidth estimate, in
practice once every few hundred milliseconds.
