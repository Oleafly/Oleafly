\begin{abstract}
\noindent
\footnotesize
Hybrid millimeter-wave receivers share one local oscillator across a large
analog array, so oscillator phase noise is common to every antenna and cannot
be averaged away by beamforming. In wideband operation the resulting impairment
is not a single common phase error: the oscillator wanders appreciably within
one orthogonal frequency division multiplexing symbol, and the intercarrier
leakage it produces is correlated across subcarriers in a way that least
squares channel estimation treats as noise. We derive the exact second-order
statistics of that leakage for a free-running oscillator with a Lorentzian
spectrum, show that they are captured by a rank-deficient covariance whose
dominant subspace has dimension approximately $2\lceil \beta T_s \rceil + 1$,
and use this structure to build \est, a variational estimator that recovers the
channel and the phase-noise trajectory jointly at a cost linear in the number
of subcarriers. Against a least squares baseline at a 3~dB corner linewidth of
200~kHz and 400~MHz of bandwidth, \est{} lowers channel estimation error by
6.8~dB and recovers 71 percent of the spectral efficiency lost to phase noise.
The gain is largest exactly where the impairment is worst, at high
constellation orders and wide bandwidth, and it collapses gracefully to the
least squares solution when the linewidth is small.
\end{abstract}
